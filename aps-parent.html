First drive: the new luxury sedan that wants to redefine the daily commute

A quieter cabin, sharper handling and a surprisingly frugal powertrain make a strong case for the segment's comeback.

By Alex Morgan 25 Nov 2026 6 min read fXin✉

Executive sedans have spent the past decade fighting for attention against a wave of SUVs. This latest model doesn't try to out-shout them — instead it doubles down on what made the class great: refinement, poise, and a sense of occasion every time you open the door.

Over two days and roughly 600 kilometres, we drove it through city traffic, winding mountain roads and long motorway stretches to find out whether it lives up to the hype.

Advertisement

Inside the cabin

The interior is a clear step forward. A single curved display spans most of the dash, yet physical controls remain for climate and volume — a welcome concession. Materials feel expensive where your hands land, and rear-seat space is generous enough for adults on long trips.

“It's the rare car that feels calmer the faster you go.”

Road and wind noise are impressively suppressed, helped by acoustic glazing and a revised door seal design. At motorway speed, conversation never needs to rise above a normal speaking voice.

On the road

Steering is light around town but builds weight naturally as speeds rise. The adaptive suspension soaks up broken surfaces without the float that plagued its predecessor, and body control on twisting roads is genuinely impressive for a car of this size.

The plug-in hybrid powertrain delivers a usable electric-only range for most commutes, switching seamlessly to petrol when needed. Over our mixed test loop we averaged better economy than the official figure suggests.

Verdict

If you've written off sedans, this might change your mind. It's comfortable, efficient and quietly confident — exactly what a daily driver should be.
